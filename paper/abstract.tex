\begin{abstract}
Does AI break the case for holding a broad capitalization-weighted index? A common claim says it does: AI shortens competitive advantage, lets firms grow without listing, and causes deflation that rewards fixed-supply assets such as Bitcoin. We examine each claim with a model in which equity value is capital plus the scarcity rents, profits above the normal return, that listed firms hold, and with data on index returns, market composition by region, index membership, real yields, and inflation regimes. Since the release of ChatGPT, capitalization-weighted indices have beaten equal-weighted and most AI-themed funds, as the model predicts: the index follows rents as they migrate, without a forecast. A smaller listed market does not lower the index return; what does is value capitalized in private hands before listing. Deflation strengthens fiat: cash earned 7\% a year in real terms in deflation years, and since its price was freed from the dollar in 1971, gold has not seen one. The rents AI attacks are listed mainly in the US; the results favor breadth, with a global all-cap index as its fullest form.
\end{abstract}
